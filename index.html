Charity Foundation CFT Tanzania

Together for wildlife conservation, stronger communities and a sustainable future.

Charity Foundation CFT Tanzania works toward wildlife and natural resource conservation, climate-smart agriculture, community empowerment, education and child protection.

What we plan to do

Our Programmes

Our eight objectives are delivered through five programme areas.

Objectives 1 & 2

Wildlife Conservation, Natural Resources and Climate Resilience

Proposed work on habitat protection, biodiversity monitoring, human-wildlife conflict mitigation and community participation in conservation.

Objectives 3 & 4

Climate-Smart Agriculture, Food Security and Rural Livelihoods

Helping farming households adopt practices that cope with a changing climate, grow more nutritious food and earn a more secure living.

Objectives 5 & 6

Economic Empowerment, Leadership and Gender Equality

Supporting women and young people with income-generating skills, and encouraging equal participation and leadership in their communities.

Objective 7

Education and Child Protection

Promoting access to quality education, environmental learning in schools, and safe environments where children are protected.

🤝
Objective 8

Vulnerable Children and Youth Support

Reaching vulnerable children and youth, including those without proper shelter and care, with support that helps them build a better future.

Why we exist

Sustainable and inclusive development

Communities across the United Republic of Tanzania face growing social, economic and environmental challenges: pollution and climate change, unsustainable use of land, forests, minerals and wildlife, limited access to quality education, and gender-based violence.

Many households also struggle with poor nutrition, disease and poverty, while women remain under-represented in leadership and more children are living on the streets. Our founding members established CFT to empower communities and promote national well-being.

  7. To promote education and child protection

Founding Members

Experienced conservationists and educators who work with communities living next to Tanzania's protected areas.

Happy Frank Mollel

Conserves wildlife through ecological monitoring and field-based assessments, and supervises tourism in protected areas. Gives conservation education to villagers next to protected areas and to students in nearby schools.

Daud Gunda

Carries out wildlife ecology and tourism work, including ecological assessment and monitoring, and produces GIS maps and spatial data for conservation planning. Develops wildlife teaching aids and gives conservation education to communities and students.

Marry Stephen

Supports wildlife conservation and habitat protection through fieldwork and community engagement, and assists in controlling problem animals. Teaches villagers and school students practices that reduce human-wildlife conflict.
